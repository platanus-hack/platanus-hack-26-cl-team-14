# Municipal vehicle-permit files in the Metropolitan Region

As of 21 November 2026, five of the 52 municipalities have a dedicated public permit file confirmed in this search. Three publish transaction data. La Reina and Maipú publish totals. **Pudahuel is the only verified source of observed vehicle colour.** For the other 47 municipalities, public file coverage remains unresolved. Permit counts describe the issuing municipality. Owners can renew outside their home comuna.

La Reina’s catalog description ends in 2015. The workbook includes a 2016 column alongside 2007–15, with quantity, percentage and value for each year. Pudahuel lists 25 resources. The 2020–23 files include vehicle type, make, model and colour. The 2022 type field includes hatchback, sedan, SUV, station wagon, pickup, van and motorcycle. The 2024 header keeps colour, make and model and drops vehicle type. The 2025 releases cover January–March together and April–December separately. The April colour header is on CSV row 20. January 2026 also contains colour values. The February–October 2026 headers omit colour. The package does not establish coverage for November or December 2026. Peñalolén’s workbook contains 187,956 payment records and five fields. It has no vehicle class or colour. The permit-year field includes past-due years, so earlier permit years do not extend the publication period.

## Search scope

The national catalog returned 257 packages for “permiso circulación” and 259 for “circulación”. I matched publisher names to the 52 Census comunas, opened the municipal resources and checked headers or workbook cells. I also searched for each municipality by name. I excluded payment instructions, individual payment lookups, municipal-fleet purchases, general annual accounts, audit reports and decrees unrelated to a released permit series. These cannot establish public file coverage. [National catalog search](https://datos.gob.cl/api/3/action/package_search?q=circulaci%C3%B3n&rows=1000)

The Council for Transparency directory lists 47 of the 52 municipalities on its central portal. All 47 municipal entries returned HTTP 403 here. The other five municipal sites opened. Melipilla’s transit index links a permit section, but the section returned a browser verification screen. Providencia’s permit-titled 2019 table contains a contract amendment. Puente Alto’s open-data portal did not establish a permit release. The remaining sites also offer no verified series from this check. These limitations prevent a conclusion about nonpublication.
