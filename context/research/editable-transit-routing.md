# Travel times for an edited transit pattern

GTFS and Conveyal represent each direction as a sequence of stop calls. Ride time belongs to the segment between calls. Wait and dwell belong to boarding and staying on board. Removing one pattern leaves other patterns on the same physical track or road available. [GTFS Schedule Reference](https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/reference/) · [Conveyal modifications](https://docs.conveyal.com/edit-scenario/modifications) · [R5 AddTrips](https://github.com/conveyal/r5/blob/dev/src/main/java/com/conveyal/r5/analyst/scenario/AddTrips.java)

The engine compares the same routing graph before and after each edit. It uses 1 km of straight-line access and egress, 300 m for walking transfers, and the catalog's first-stop start rate for Monday from 08:00 to 09:00. Half the rate-derived interval is a wait estimate. It cannot establish the next vehicle time at a downstream stop. Patterns with no starts in that window are outside the graph.

Each result gives travel minutes between comuna population points. A null time means the graph did not find a path with at least one stop-to-stop ride. The scenario can apply a change to calibrated public transport times when both graph times are finite. This layer makes no estimate of a line's passenger count. A geometry-only move keeps the same time and stop order.

I checked the module with the March GTFS catalog in #243. A hypothetical 14-minute directional service from Plaza de Maipú to Tobalaba, every five minutes, changed 26 comuna pairs. Moving its drawing changed zero time pairs. Removing it reversed all 26 changes. Submitting the same ordered stops and times for an L1 pattern kept its complete feature and returned zero changed pairs. Removing bus pattern `101:0:101I:bca9b5d49c23a375` changed five pairs. This check did not run HTTP, MCP, or the viewer.
