# Post-match travel from the Monumental and MUT

The pitch compares the same match in two locations. The current [event projection](../../engine/src/attendance.rs) estimates demand for the whole return period. It transfers weekday 08:00 Metro access to that period. Changing the event end from 20:00 to 23:00 does not change the service used in the estimate.

## Evidence

- A Paris 2024 study linked 207 events to 309 station entry profiles at 13 stations. It used 30-minute counts from one hour before each event ended to three hours after. Use the observation method. Local match counts are needed to fit the profile for Santiago. [hEART study, sections 2–3](https://transp-or.epfl.ch/heart/2025/abstracts/hEART_2025_shortpaper_259.pdf)
- Red Movilidad announced a 211X reinforcement near the Monumental for Colo-Colo versus Universidad Católica on 22 November 2026. The buses were scheduled for 20:00–21:00 from two boarding points toward San Bernardo. At the same stadium on 11 October 2024, a concert plan extended entry at Pedrero and San Joaquín to 01:30. These are service plans. They give no observed passenger counts. [Match plan](https://www.red.cl/red-comunica/liga-de-primera-red-movilidad-refuerza-el-recorrido-211-para-el-partido-entre-colo-colo-y-universidad-catolica-en-el-estadio-monumental/) · [Concert plan](https://www.red.cl/red-comunica/red-movilidad-y-metro-despliegan-un-plan-especial-de-transporte-para-concierto-de-paul-mccartney-en-el-estadio-monumental/)
- MATSim tracks passengers waiting at stops and records when a vehicle is full and a passenger cannot board. This separates arrival at a stop from boarding a vehicle. The FHWA handbook uses queue length and clearance time to evaluate event plans. [MATSim book, ch. 13](https://matsim.org/files/book/partOne-latest.pdf) · [MATSim stop tracker source](https://matsim.org/doxygen/_transit_stop_agent_tracker_8java_source.html) · [FHWA measures](https://ops-dr.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwaop04010/chapter5_02.htm)

## First layer and check

1. Start with a post-match station entry profile. Hold attendance and the origin mix fixed between venues. Apply the profile after the end time and account for walking to the station. Use dated service for that window, including any event reinforcement.
2. Fit on one observed match. Freeze the model, then compare the half-hour excess entries at Pedrero and Mirador on a different match against a matched non-match day. Compare the sum of absolute bin errors with a baseline that puts all event entries in the first half hour. Report the peak bin and total excess entries. Keep signed observed differences, including negative bins. Report closures and lost observations.
3. Add a queue only when there is effective capacity or queue evidence. At a station entrance, serve up to the observed gate throughput and carry the rest to the next interval. On a platform, available places equal train capacity less passengers remaining after alighting. Separate the two queues. Without queue observations, report station entries alone. A gate validation does not measure train boarding.

The [DTPM matrices](https://www.dtpm.cl/index.php/documentos/matrices-de-viaje) give sampled periods and station or stop records. Match the exact date against the sample before scoring. The [repo capacity notes](../../data/mvp/metro_capacity.md) explain why scheduled departures and nominal train places cannot establish spare boarding capacity. The [26 April 2025 match comparison in PR #214](https://github.com/platanus-hack/platanus-hack-26-cl-team-14/pull/214) gives a local starting point: Pedrero recorded 2,620 entries and Mirador 1,596 from 18:00 to 19:00. Use it to check the timing and scale of total entries. The next day is a Sunday and cannot establish the non-match Saturday baseline. A second match and comparable Saturdays are still needed for the proposed score. Until those counts are available, the clock and any queues shown in the pitch represent assumptions.
